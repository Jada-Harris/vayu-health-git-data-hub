Data Dictionary / Clinical outcomes

HbA1c / GMI baseline

Metric HbA1c / GMI baseline

In plain terms

A member's starting A1c (or GMI) is the reading closest to their enrollment start date, preferring readings taken before enrollment.

Business definition

  1. Among valued HbA1c or 1-month GMI strictly before ECM enrollment start, pick the closest to enrollment (smallest absolute day gap). Do not compare measurement values.
  2. If none before enrollment, among valued HbA1c / GMI on or after enrollment, pick the closest to enrollment.
  3. Same-distance ties prefer HbA1c over GMI.

Once a baseline (value, date, marker) is filled on the MART / master row, monthly runs do not overwrite it (fill blank only). Same lock applies to PHQ-9 / DDS / GAD-7 / PAM Level / PAM Score baselines.

Terms used on this page

  • A1c: A blood test that shows a person's average blood sugar over about the last three months. Also written HbA1c. Higher numbers mean less controlled diabetes.
  • DDS: Diabetes Distress Scale: a short survey about how stressful living with diabetes feels.
  • ECM: Enhanced Care Management: the Medi-Cal (CalAIM) benefit that pays Vayu to coordinate care for members with complex needs.
  • GAD-7: A 7-question anxiety screening survey.
  • GMI: Glucose Management Indicator: an estimate of A1c calculated from CGM readings.
  • HbA1c: Same as A1c.
  • MART: The BigQuery layer of analysis-ready tables, such as one row per member or per member per month.
  • PAM: Patient Activation Measure: a 13-question survey (scored 0 to 100, levels 1 to 4) about how ready a person is to manage their own health.
  • PHQ-9: A 9-question depression screening survey.
